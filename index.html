How to solve arithmetic expressions by hand

  1. Work inside brackets first.
  2. Resolve exponents and roots next.
  3. Perform multiplication and division, left to right.
  4. Finish with addition and subtraction, left to right.

This order — brackets, exponents, multiplication/division, addition/subtraction — is usually remembered as BODMAS or PEMDAS. Getting the order wrong is the single most common arithmetic mistake, so the solver above always follows it exactly.

Linear vs. quadratic equations

A linear equation has x to the power of 1 only (like 2x+5=15) and always has exactly one solution, found by isolating x on one side.

A quadratic equation has x² as its highest power (like x²−5x+6=0) and has up to two solutions, found with the quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a, where a, b and c are the coefficients.

Differentiation vs. integration

Differentiation finds the rate of change of a function — the slope of its graph at any point. Integration reverses this process, finding the original function (the antiderivative) from its rate of change, plus an unknown constant C since any constant disappears when differentiated.

Frequently asked questions

Is MathSolve free to use?

Yes. Every solver on this page — arithmetic, algebra, trigonometry, calculus and statistics — is free with no sign-up required.

Does it actually calculate the answer, or just guess?

It performs a real calculation using a math engine in your browser. The steps shown reflect the method actually used to reach the answer, not a generated description.

What grade levels does it cover?

From basic grade-school arithmetic through algebra and trigonometry in secondary school, up to calculus and statistics taught at college and postgraduate level.

Can it solve calculus integrals with trigonometric or exponential terms?

The integral solver currently handles polynomial terms (like 3x²+4x−7) precisely. The derivative solver handles a much wider range, including trigonometric, exponential and product expressions.

Why does my equation show "no real solution"?

Some quadratic equations have roots that are not real numbers (they involve the square root of a negative number). The solver will tell you when this happens instead of showing an incorrect result.
